# The Rocky model

How the simulation model is built and what is in it. The gait and the RL task
are documented in [gait.md](gait.md) and the [top-level README](../README.md).

Exported from the Fusion 360 master model `Rocky`. Everything in `model/` is
generated by `model/build_model.py` from the CAD export in `link_data.json`;
nothing is hand-tuned geometry.

```
model/
  rocky.urdf              URDF (Isaac Lab, pinocchio, ROS)
  rocky.xml               MJCF, mjlab-ready: no <actuator>, one "home" keyframe
  rocky_standalone.xml    rocky.xml + floor, light, position servos (plain MuJoCo)
  meshes/*.stl            per-part visual meshes, metres, in link frames
  meshes/hull_*.stl       per-link convex collision hulls
  link_data.json          raw Fusion export: frames, per-body volumes and CoMs
  build_model.py          regenerates the URDF/MJCF/hulls from link_data.json
```

Quick look:

```bash
python -m mujoco.viewer --mjcf=model/rocky_standalone.xml   # Ctrl+L, load key "home"
```

`src/rocky/model_params.py` restates the geometry and actuator constants for the
gait and the RL task; `tests/test_model.py` asserts every one of them against the
compiled MJCF, so the two cannot drift apart.

## Kinematics

Z up. The base frame origin is the **bottom face of the chassis, on the body
axis** — the same origin as the CAD. Leg *k* is mounted at 72·k°, with **leg 0 on
+X being the manipulator limb**. Each leg's frame is rotated by its mount angle,
so within a leg: **+X points radially outward, lift/elbow rotate about the leg's
local Y**.

| Chain | Offset from parent |
|---|---|
| base → coxa_k | 101.127 mm radial, 22 mm up, yaw 72·k° |
| coxa_k → femur_k | 42 mm along leg +X |
| femur_k → tibia_k | 130 mm along femur +X |
| tibia_0 → gripper_0 | 90 mm along tibia +X |
| tibia_k → foot sphere | 162.3 mm along tibia +X, r = 12 mm |
| gripper_0 → foot sphere | 74.4 mm along gripper +X, r = 10 mm |

Joint zero is the **straight leg**: femur radially outward and horizontal, tibia
in line with the femur. Positive lift/elbow rotate about +Y, which swings +X
downward — so the standing pose is lift −50° (femur up) and elbow +122° (tibia
down and out).

16 actuated joints: 5 × sweep, 5 × lift, 5 × elbow, 1 × wrist. The gripper jaws
are welded shut and the rubber pad under them is the fifth foot, as designed.

In the home pose: belly 45 mm off the ground, knees 167 mm up, footprint
555 mm across, whole-robot CoM 100 mm up and 3.5 mm toward the manipulator leg.

**Total 4.170 kg**, of which 0.430 kg is declared payload: 0.330 kg battery at
z = 64 mm (the CAD battery envelope on the deck) and 0.100 kg of boards and
wiring at z = 30 mm. Structure + actuators alone is 3.740 kg.

Densities: printed parts PETG 1.27 g/cm³ at 100 % infill (real prints will come
in 5–10 % lighter), TPU 1.20 for the shoulder boots and foot pads, nylon 1.15
for servo horns. Fixed masses: STS3215 60 g each (17 modelled, 16 driven),
F6700ZZ bearings 4.3 g, foot micro-switches 0.3 g. Inertia tensors are computed
from the mesh of every individual body at its own density and summed per link —
not from a uniform-density assumption over the whole link.

## Collision

- One convex hull per link (`meshes/hull_*.stl`), named `<body>_collision`.
- One sphere per foot, `foot_k_collision`, sized and placed so its lowest point
  is exactly the CAD pad contact point — all five level in the home pose.
- The claw and pad geometry is excluded from the tibia/gripper hulls so the foot
  sphere is the only contact there.
- In the mjlab config the hulls are `condim=1` (frictionless) and the feet are
  `condim=6, priority=1` with friction `1 / 5e-3 / 5e-4`, the Go1 scheme: shins
  and belly can catch on terrain but only the feet provide traction.

## Actuators

Feetech STS3215 at 12 V, modelled as MuJoCo `position` servos:

| | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| effort limit | 2.9 N·m | 30 kgf·cm stall |
| velocity limit | 4.7 rad/s | 0.222 s / 60° no-load |
| gear ratio | 345 | STS3215 |
| rotor inertia | 5.0e-8 kg·m² | **assumed** — no published figure |
| armature (reflected) | 5.95e-3 kg·m² | rotor × gear² |
| kp / kv | 23.5 / 1.50 | 10 Hz, ζ = 2 (mjlab's formula) |
| frictionloss | 0.05 N·m | assumed gearbox stiction |

Standing costs 0.76 N·m at the lift joint and 0.39 N·m at the elbow — 26 % of
stall — and the servo compliance lets the body settle 5.7 mm with ~1.9° of lift
droop. That softness is real for these servos; if it looks too slack, the honest
knob is `ROTOR_INERTIA` (it moves armature, kp and kv together).

`ROCKY_ACTION_SCALE` is set explicitly (sweep 0.25, lift/elbow 0.40, wrist 0.30
rad) rather than from mjlab's `0.25 · effort / stiffness` heuristic, which yields
0.031 rad here — a 1.8° command envelope, unusable for walking. The heuristic
suits quasi-direct-drive motors, not a 1:345 hobby servo.

## Notes and known simplifications

- The spring-loaded foot plunger is **rigid** here: no slider joint, no contact
  switch. Foot contact comes from the MuJoCo contact sensor instead.
- Gripper jaw sliders and the 17th servo are omitted from the DOF list; the
  gripper servo's mass is carried in the `gripper_0` link.
- Loading `rocky.urdf` directly in MuJoCo welds `base_link` into the world body
  (URDF has no floating joint), which drops the base inertial. Use `rocky.xml`
  for MuJoCo; the URDF is for tools that add their own floating base.
- URDF and MJCF were cross-checked: identical masses and inertias, and body
  positions agree to 1.4e-17 m in the home pose.
- Mesh tolerance 0.25 mm (0.5 mm for the carapace); mesh volumes are within 1.5 %
  of the Fusion solids. Hulls are decimated on a 3 mm grid, which grows them by
  up to 7 % in volume — deliberately slightly conservative for collision.
- Joint ranges are the mechanical stops from the CAD, unchanged.

## Regenerating after a CAD change

Re-run the two Fusion export scripts (they write `link_data.json` and
`meshes/*.stl` into `model/`), then:

```bash
python model/build_model.py && pytest tests/test_model.py
```

`tests/test_model.py` is the regression net: joint ranges, link offsets, masses,
foot spheres, the home keyframe, URDF/MJCF equivalence and a two-second standing
test all run against the freshly generated files.
